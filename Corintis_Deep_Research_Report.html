2) Technology & Advantages

Microfluidic “in‑chip” / co‑packaged cooling

Coolant flows through micro‑channels etched in silicon or a bonded layer, bringing liquid directly under hotspots, eliminating thermal interfaces and large temperature deltas vs. traditional cold plates.

AI‑optimized hot‑spot targeting

Glacierware software co‑designs channel networks to each chip’s power map (bio‑inspired “vein” topologies), allocating flow where it matters; paired with rapid thermal emulation (“Therminator”).

Measured / reported benefits

  • Up to 3× heat removal vs. best cold plates in prototypes; GPU peak silicon temperature reduced by ~65%.
  • Higher sustained clocks / reduced thermal throttling; overclocking headroom under bursty workloads.
  • Lower pressure drop → less pump energy; efficient flow distribution (no over‑cooling of cool regions).
  • Warm‑water operation (e.g., 40–45 °C inlets; ~70 °C outlets): less chiller energy, easier heat reuse.
  • Potentially **orders‑of‑magnitude** improvement in cooling energy efficiency vs. legacy baselines.

Manufacturability

Fine‑feature copper/silicon structures at scale; sealed cap designs; rapid iteration proven with hyperscaler R&D (multiple prototype cycles in under a year).

Note: Regulations focus on outcomes (safety, energy, water) rather than cooling method. Corintis’s approach generally supports compliance trends.
